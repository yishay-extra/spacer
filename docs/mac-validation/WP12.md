# WP12 — manual validation of external clocks on a real Mac

CI covers everything that needs no gear: the clock maths with synthetic,
jittered input (`tests/TimeDllTests.cpp`, `MidiClockTrackerTests.cpp`,
`MtcTests.cpp`, `OscBeatTrackerTests.cpp`, `ClockFollowerTests.cpp`). That
includes MIDI clock locking within two bars at 60–300 bpm with ±1 ms
timestamp jitter, staying within 1 ms over 10 simulated minutes with 80 ppm
sender / 40 ppm device drift, relocking after a tempo jump, and MTC at all
four rates. CI also covers the engine side (`tests/engine/ExternalClockTests.cpp`):
switching clocks through `SwapGraph`, the hand-over on rebuilds, recalls and
device restarts, a MidiClock fed from a second thread with no audio-thread
allocation, and the OSC receive-thread tap.

This checklist covers real senders: a DAW, a MIDI interface or IAC bus, a
second machine, and a Link peer. Record pass / fail plus notes for each.

There is no clock-source UI yet (Settings is a later WP), so choose the
source in the project file and open it: `"clock": {"kind": "Midi", "bpm": 120,
"beatsPerBar": 4}`. `kind` is one of `Internal`, `Midi`, `Mtc`, `Osc` or
`Link`. The status bar shows bpm, bar.beat and, for an external source,
"MIDI clock (locked)" / "(unlocked)". If it says "external clock unavailable",
the app fell back to the internal clock (for example Link without
`SPACER_WITH_LINK`).

## Setup

1. A Mac on macOS 12+ with the Spacer app built in Release. Use a project with
   one source on an `Orbit` motion with `period {"beats": 4}` (one turn per
   bar, so lock is visible and audible), two or more speakers, and a cue list
   with an `AtBeat` cue at bar 5 beat 1.
2. A DAW that can send MIDI clock and MTC (Logic, Ableton Live, Reaper), to
   the macOS IAC Driver ("Audio MIDI Setup → MIDI Studio → IAC Driver → Device
   is online") or to a hardware MIDI interface looped back.
3. For OSC: a second machine or a script (`python-osc`) that sends
   `/spacer/clock/beat f` at 20–60 Hz.
4. For Link: a build configured with `-DSPACER_WITH_LINK=ON` (see the licence
   note in the WP12 PR), and Ableton Live or the LinkHut example on the same
   network.

## MIDI clock (`kind: "Midi"`)

1. DAW at 120 bpm, sending clock to the IAC bus, transport stopped. Open the
   project. Expected: within a second the status bar shows 120.0 bpm and
   "(locked)", with bar.beat not moving.
2. Press play in the DAW. Expected: Spacer starts at 1.1 at the same moment,
   the orbit turns once per bar in time with the DAW's metronome, and
   "(locked)" appears within **two bars** of the start.
3. Stop, locate the DAW to bar 9, and play again. Expected: Spacer resumes at
   9.1 (Song Position Pointer + Continue).
4. Change the DAW tempo from 120 to 140 while playing. Expected: Spacer
   follows within about two bars, with no jump backwards.
5. Let it play for 10 minutes. Record a click from the DAW and a Spacer test
   tone that fires on beat 1 of each bar (for example an `AtBeat` cue that
   recalls a scene with a short click source). Expected: the offset stays
   constant within about 1 ms after the first bars. A constant offset is
   output latency, which this WP does not calibrate (see the PR).
6. Unplug or disable the clock while playing. Expected: the beat stops
   advancing and the status shows "(unlocked)" within 0.5 s. Re-enable it and
   the clock relocks.
7. The `AtBeat` cue at bar 5 beat 1 fires exactly on the DAW's bar 5.

## MTC (`kind: "Mtc"`, `bpm` set to the DAW's tempo)

1. DAW sending MTC at 25 fps from 01:00:00:00. Press play. Expected: Spacer
   starts within ~2 frames (80 ms). The beat is timecode seconds × bpm / 60,
   so 01:00:00:00 at 120 bpm is beat 7200 (bar 1801).
2. Repeat at 24, 29.97 drop-frame and 30 fps. Expected: no drift against the
   DAW over 5 minutes.
3. Locate the DAW while playing. Expected: Spacer jumps to the new position
   within two frames. Locate while stopped (the DAW sends a full-frame
   message). Expected: the position updates without starting.
4. Stop. Expected: Spacer stops within 4 frames.

## OSC (`kind: "Osc"`)

1. Send `/spacer/clock/beat` with an advancing beat at 120 bpm, 30 times a
   second. Expected: Spacer plays, the status bar shows ~120 bpm, and the
   orbit is smooth (no stepping at the message rate).
2. Send the same beat twice in a row, or stop sending. Expected: stopped
   (immediately, or after 1 s).
3. Send a beat 16 beats ahead. Expected: Spacer jumps there.
4. With `kind: "Internal"`, `/spacer/clock/beat 8` still sets the internal
   clock's beat (WP8 behaviour).

## Ableton Link (`kind: "Link"`, Link build only)

1. Start Live (Link enabled) at 128 bpm and open the project. Expected:
   "Link (locked)" once a peer is present; Spacer shows 128 bpm.
2. Start the transport in Live (Start/Stop Sync enabled in Live's Link
   preferences). Expected: Spacer starts in phase, with bar boundaries
   aligned to Live's (quantum = `beatsPerBar`).
3. Run for **10 minutes**, recording Live's click and a Spacer bar click as
   in MIDI step 5. Expected: within **1 ms** of a constant offset (PLAN WP12
   acceptance).
4. Change tempo in Live. Expected: Spacer follows at once.

## Switching

1. Edit the project file from `Midi` to `Internal` and reopen it while
   playing. Expected: the internal clock continues from the beat the
   external clock was at (no jump).
2. Edit a source (add one) while an external clock runs. Expected: the clock
   stays locked; the rebuilt graph inherits the clock.
